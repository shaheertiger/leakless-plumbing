Don't Let It Grow

When a Small Job Is Warning You About a Bigger One

Most expensive plumbing emergencies started as something small that seemed ignorable. These are the ones worth calling about sooner rather than later.

A Toilet That Clogs Repeatedly

A one-off clog is normal. The same toilet clogging every week usually means a partial blockage further down the line, a venting problem, or roots in the main — none of which a plunger fixes.

A Slow Drain That Keeps Coming Back

If a drain clears then slows again within weeks, the blockage is not being removed, only pushed. See drain cleaning — clearing it properly once costs less than clearing it badly four times.

A Cabinet Floor That Stays Damp

A slow leak under a sink can rot a cabinet base and the subfloor beneath it long before anyone notices. Damp particleboard is a repair; rotted subfloor is a renovation.

A Water Bill That Jumped

A silently running toilet or a hidden supply leak can add a startling amount to a bill. If nothing about your usage changed, something is leaking — see leak detection.

A Shut-Off Valve That Will Not Turn

You find out a valve is seized at the worst possible moment — mid-emergency, with water spraying. Replacing a stuck valve on a calm Tuesday is a small job; needing it during a burst line is not.

Gurgling From Another Fixture

If flushing a toilet makes the tub gurgle, that is air being pulled through a partially blocked drain or vent. It is an early warning of a main-line problem, and it will not resolve itself.

Before You Reach for the Drain Cleaner

When DIY Is Worth Trying, and When It Costs You More

Some small plumbing jobs are genuinely worth attempting yourself: plunging a toilet properly, swapping a showerhead, replacing a toilet flapper, tightening a slip nut on a P-trap. If it goes wrong, the worst case is usually a wet floor and a call to a plumber anyway.

Where it turns costly is chemical drain cleaner and force. Caustic cleaners sit on a blockage they cannot dissolve, damaging older pipes and creating a hazard for whoever opens that line later. Over-tightening plastic fittings cracks them. And forcing a hardware-store auger into a toilet regularly chips or cracks the porcelain, turning a modest unclogging into a full replacement. If the plunger has not worked twice, stop and call. Related: drain cleaning for stubborn or recurring blockages, and affordable plumbing for how we price work generally.

Small Jobs FAQ

Small Job Questions, Answered

Wondering whether your job is too small to bother us with? It almost certainly is not. Call 437-996-1996 and ask.

Will a plumber come out for just one small job?

Yes. A single running toilet, one dripping tap, one seized valve — those are normal calls for us. The only thing worth knowing is that there is a base charge for coming out at all, which is why bundling several small items into one visit gives you much better value. Call 437-996-1996 and list everything you need done.

How much does a cheap plumber charge to unclog a toilet?

It depends on where the blockage is. A clog in the toilet trap itself is quick. One further down the branch line, or a toilet that needs to be pulled off the flange to reach an object, takes longer and costs more. Tell us on the phone whether other fixtures are also draining slowly — that single detail usually tells us which of the two you are dealing with.

What does it cost to have a plumber install a toilet?

A straight swap where the existing flange and shut-off valve are in good condition is a short, predictable job. The cost rises if the flange is broken or sits at the wrong height, if the shut-off valve is seized and needs replacing, or if the floor underneath has been damaged by a long-term leak. Mention the age of the toilet and whether the floor around its base is soft when you call, and we will price it accordingly.

Can I bundle several small jobs into one visit?

Yes, and you should. The fixed cost of a visit is the same whether the plumber does one job or five, so bundling is the single most effective way to keep small-job plumbing inexpensive. Walk the house before you call and write down every drip, wobble, and slow drain.

Should I just try to fix it myself?

For a plunge, a flapper swap, or a showerhead, go ahead. Avoid chemical drain cleaners — they can damage older pipes and create a hazard for whoever opens that line next — and avoid forcing a hardware-store auger into a toilet, which is a common way to crack the porcelain and turn a small job into a replacement. If two honest attempts have not fixed it, call 437-996-1996.

Do you do small jobs for landlords and property managers?

Yes. Multi-unit small-repair lists are some of our most efficient visits, because everything is grouped into one trip. See our residential plumbing and commercial plumbing pages, and call with your list.
